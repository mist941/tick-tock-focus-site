How it works

  1. Build a preset

    Name it and add up to 50 steps, each from 30 seconds to just under 24 hours. Drag them, or use the arrow buttons, to put them in order.

  2. Start it in one click

    Pick the preset and press Start; next time, it's already selected. Close the popup and the timer keeps going.

  3. Get notified at every step

    A Chrome notification appears as each step ends. The last one tells you the whole routine is done.

Features

  • Multi-step presets

    Up to 50 steps per preset, each from 30 seconds to just under 24 hours. Reorder them by dragging or with the arrow buttons.

  • A notification at every step

    Chrome lets you know as each step ends and when the routine is done, so you can stop checking the clock.

  • Keeps running in the background

    Close the popup or restart Chrome and the timer keeps its schedule. If a routine ended while Chrome was closed, you get one notification with the time it finished.

  • Presets that follow you

    With Chrome Sync on, your presets and settings reach your other computers through your Google account. They never pass through a server of ours, because there isn't one.

  • Progress at a glance

    The popup shows a countdown, a progress ring with a marker between steps, and a clock you can switch between 12- and 24-hour time.

  • Keyboard and screen reader friendly

    Labeled controls, full keyboard support with arrow buttons as an alternative to dragging, and status messages announced to screen readers.

Private by design

TickTockFocus uses three Chrome permissions and nothing else. It makes no network requests of its own, and there's no account or analytics.

Permissions it uses

storage
Saves your presets, your clock format, and the state of the running timer.
alarms
Wakes the extension when a step ends, even when the popup is closed.
notifications
Shows a notification as each step ends. It's the only permission Chrome lists when you add the extension: “Display notifications.”

What it never asks for

  • Access to the websites you visit
  • Your browsing history
  • The content of any page you open
  • A sign-in or account
